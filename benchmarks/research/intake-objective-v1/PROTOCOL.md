## Why

`brief.objective` is the first ask in input order. The intake-dictation-v1 results name this as a known limit: in
the owner's 317-token dictation the first ask is a secondary sentence, and that benchmark does not measure which
ask is the main one ([RESULTS.md](../intake-dictation-v1/RESULTS.md), "Known limit").

The longer the dictation, the worse the rule gets. A long dictation holds one or two real requests and many small
connected pieces: context, side requests, thinking aloud, asides. Almost every sentence carries a request cue, so
"first ask" picks whatever came first. This study asks whether a deterministic ranking of topic blocks picks the
main ask more often, and whether one Jev judgment over the few best blocks adds anything on top.

What it cannot show: how often real dictations bury the main ask, or how the ranking behaves on another speaker.
The generator and the ranking come from the same author (see Leakage controls); Part R is the only real data and it
is small.

## Questions

- **Q1.** Does the deterministic ranking (arm D) pick the main ask more often than the first-ask rule (A0)?
- **Q2.** Does one Jev choice over D's top blocks (arm DJ) pick it more often than D?
- **Q3.** How small is the brief (objective plus the blocks linked to it) next to the input, and does every gold
  ask stay recoverable (in the brief or in a stash that rebuilds byte for byte)?

## Data

### Part S: synthetic, answer known by construction

Generated by `intake_objective_v1.py generate` with `random.Random(20261116)`.

- **Bases.** 40 hand-written main requests, 20 pt-BR and 20 English. Each has one main ask sentence and up to two
  supporting sentences (gold: linked to the main ask).
- **Block pool.** Hand-written micro-contexts of 2 to 5 sentences, each on one topic, of four kinds: narrative
  context with no ask, a secondary ask (a real request of lower stakes, such as "e manda um oi pro João"), thinking
  aloud, and an aside to someone in the room.
- **Families.** Each family is a scenario built around one signal. A family can carry more than one signal (a
  restatement also repeats the topic's words), so family results describe scenarios; which signal D depends on is
  read from the ablations under Arms.

| Family | How the main ask stands out | Gold main |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `marker` | the speaker names it ("o principal é", "the main thing is") | the marked ask |
| `recurrence` | its topic comes back in two or three later blocks, without restating the ask | the base's ask |
| `restate` | the speaker repeats it at the end ("então resumindo, ...") | the first statement and its restatement both count |
| `first` | it is the first ask (A0's easy case, the non-inferiority control) | the base's ask |
| `bare` | no surface signal; the other asks are trivial errands | the base's ask |

- **Length.** k = 4, 8 or 16 blocks around the base, about 150 to 1,500 words. The base sits at a random block
  position, except in `first` (position 0) and `restate` (statement random, restatement last).
- **Size.** 40 bases × 5 families × 3 lengths = 600 cases. Odd-numbered bases are the **dev** split (the ranking
  may be tuned on them); even-numbered bases are the **test** split, and the headline numbers are test numbers.

### Part R: the owner's real dictations

20 to 30 dictations of 300 words or more that the owner picks. Before any arm runs on them, the owner marks the main
ask sentence (or sentences) in each one. They stay outside the repository (`~/.lcc-private/intake-objective-v1/`);
only aggregate numbers are published. No Part R text goes to Jev without the owner's explicit yes.

## Arms

- **A0.** Current `brief.objective`: the first ask in input order.
- **A1.** The last ask in input order. A cheap control: if A1 matches D, position alone explains D.
- **D (deterministic).** Three steps, all offline:
  1. *Blocks.* Adjacent sentences are grouped into blocks. A block ends at a topic-shift marker ("outra coisa",
     "voltando", "another thing", "anyway") or where two neighbouring sentences share no content word.
  2. *Score.* Each block gets a score from four signals: recurrence (BM25 of the block's terms against the other
     blocks, the scorer already in `lcc.relevance.specialist`), a priority marker, a restatement near the end, and
     the existing ask cues. The weights are tuned on dev only and frozen in an amendment before the test run.
  3. *Pick.* Only blocks holding at least one sentence with the role `ask` are ranked; a block of context or
     recurring mentions alone can never be the objective. The objective is the first ask sentence of the top
     eligible block. When no block is eligible, D returns A0's objective. Blocks sharing content terms with the
     objective are linked; the rest go to the stash with an `lcc recall` pointer.
- **Ablations of D.** D with one signal removed at a time (no recurrence, no marker, no restatement, no ask cues),
  same weights otherwise. Offline and free; reported on test next to D, with no pass criterion.
- **DJ (D plus Jev, opt-in).** One System One `choice` over D's top 5 eligible blocks: which block holds the main
  request. Jev's pick is used at p >= 0.6; below that, or when Jev is missing or fails, DJ returns D's pick. One
  call per case, whatever the length. DJ returns one pick, not a ranking, so it is scored on hit@1 only.

## Scoring (mechanical, no judge)

Gold is carried as character spans of the generated text. An arm's objective hits when its span overlaps a gold
main span by at least half of the gold span.

## Metrics

- **hit@1** per arm: the objective hits.
- **hit@3** for D only: a gold main block is among its top 3 eligible blocks.
- **ask recall:** gold asks (main and secondary) present in `brief.asks` or in the stash; the stash must rebuild the
  input byte for byte.
- **brief ratio:** tokens of objective plus linked blocks over input tokens (heuristic counter, as in the gate).
- **link precision and recall** against the gold supporting sentences.
- **cost:** wall time of D per case, and Jev calls for DJ.

All metrics by family and length, plus the test-split total.

## Hypotheses and pass criteria (fixed by the design approval of 2026-11-15)

The 300 test cases are 15 variants of 20 bases, so they are not independent. Every interval below resamples whole
bases (paired cluster bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, seed 20261116), never single cases.

- **H1.** On test, D hit@1 minus A0 hit@1 is at least 0.15 and the lower bound of its 95% cluster interval is
  above 0.
- **H2.** On test, DJ hit@1 minus D hit@1 has the lower bound of its 95% cluster interval above 0. If H2 fails, DJ
  is not shipped and D stays the default.
- **H3.** In the `first` family, D hit@1 is no lower than A0 hit@1 minus 0.05 (D must not break the easy case).
- **H4.** Ask recall is 1.00 in every arm, and every stash rebuilds byte for byte.
- **Part R** is reported with Wilson 95% intervals and no pass criterion: about 25 dictations give a half-width
  near 0.19, so it shows a direction, not a verdict.
- **Power.** Twenty test bases is the unit of inference, so only a large gain can clear H1 and H2. A null result
  means "not shown at this size", not "no effect".

**The frozen D.**

- Weights: recurrence 0.0, marker 0.5, restate 0.5, ask 0.5 (`WEIGHTS`). The score is linear, so any positive
  multiple of these weights ranks the blocks the same way.
- How they were chosen: `run_arms.py tune` scores every combination of 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 per signal on dev and ranks
  them by hit@1, then hit@3, then the smallest total weight. The ten best all reach hit@1 268/300 and hit@3 298/300,
  all with recurrence at 0; the first is frozen.
- Guard: `run_arms.py run --split test` refuses unless `objective.py` hashes to `FROZEN_OBJECTIVE_SHA256` and D's
  dev output hashes to `FROZEN_D_SHA256`; it checks both before D runs on any test case.
  `tests/test_intake_objective.py` fails if a later change moves either hash. The library files D imports are pinned
  by the table above and, for any change that alters D's behaviour on dev, by the output hash.
- Two fixes from the code review came after the tuning: an input with no sentence keeps its whitespace, and ask cues
  are counted per sentence. D's dev picks are the same before and after them, so the tuning stands.

**How D fills in what the design left open.**

1. Content words: accent-folded, four letters or more, outside the parser's pt-BR and English stopwords, cut to
   their first five letters.
2. Blocks as designed: a new block starts at a topic-shift cue or where a sentence shares no content word with the
   one before it. Block spans tile the input: leading whitespace joins the first block, the whitespace after a
   block belongs to it, and an input with no sentence (empty or whitespace) is one block with no sentence.
3. Signals, each scaled to [0, 1] within one input:
   - recurrence: the block's BM25 against every other block, summed, over the largest such sum in the input;
   - marker: 1 when the block matches a priority cue;
   - restate: when the first sentence of the last block matches a recap cue, the recap block gets 1 and each
     earlier block its BM25 against the recap, over the largest;
   - ask: the count of the parser's ask cues in the block's sentences, each sentence read on its own, over the
     largest.
4. Ties go to the earlier block, the order the first-ask rule uses.
5. Links: a block other than the objective's own is linked when it shares a content word with the objective
   sentence.
6. The author wrote the topic-shift, priority and recap cue lists after reading the dev phrasings; the test
   phrasings stay unread.

**Recurrence gets weight 0.** Before the freeze, these variants ran on dev:

- content words of three letters instead of four;
- a block rule that joins a sentence sharing a word with any sentence of the current block;
- recurrence counted over non-adjacent blocks only;
- raw BM25 sums, or counts of blocks sharing a word, in place of scaling by the largest value.

None gave recurrence a positive weight at the best hit@1, which stayed at 268/300 in every variant. With recurrence
forced above 0 the best was 248 to 259. In the six normalisation variants, broken down by family, those weights
lifted the `recurrence` family to as much as 55/60 from 43/60 and dropped `first` to between 40 and 49 from 60, and
`bare` to between 40 and 42 from 47. The dev cases show why. D cuts finer blocks than the generator (24.8 per case
against 10.5), so the sentences of one pool block land in separate blocks and score recurrence off each other, and
unrelated pool blocks share everyday words. Scaling by the largest value lifts that noise to 1 in cases with no real
recurrence. A base's supporting sentences often share no content word with its ask: in 132 of the 300 dev cases (9
of the 20 dev bases) the main ask is a block on its own. D keeps the designed rules and signal with weight 0; the
no-recurrence ablation therefore equals D by construction, and it is still reported, as the design asks.

**Metric definitions** where the design was open or inconsistent.

- *Ask recall.* Q3 says "in the brief or in a stash"; the Metrics list says "`brief.asks` or in the stash". The
  study uses Q3's reading: a gold ask counts when the objective's block, a linked block or a stash block covers at
  least half of it. D's blocks tile the input, so D's recall is 1.00 whenever its stash rebuilds. A0 and A1 change
  only which sentence is the objective and keep the intake's sentence list, so the same holds for them. The other
  reading is reported apart as `asks_labelled`: the share of gold asks the parser labels `ask`, which is what
  reaches `brief.asks`. Every arm shares the parser, so it is one number for all arms; on dev it is 0.68.
- *Rebuild.* The objective's block, the linked blocks and the stash blocks, joined in input order, equal the input
  byte for byte, with no block lost.
- *Brief ratio.* `approximate_token_count` of the objective's block plus the linked blocks, over the input's.
- *Links.* Precision and recall against the gold supporting sentences, on the cases where D hits. A linked sentence
  is any sentence of the objective's block or of a linked block, except the objective and the gold main sentences.
- *hit@3.* The span of one of D's top three eligible blocks covers at least half of a gold main span.
- *Fallback.* With no eligible block D returns `brief.objective`, the opening of the input rather than one sentence;
  it is scored with span (0, 0), a miss. Every Part S case has a main ask the parser labels `ask` (the bench tests
  check it), so the fallback cannot happen in Part S. It can in Part R.
- *Cost.* Wall time of `analyse` plus `choose` per case, parsing included, on the owner's Mac.

2. State `{"message": <the whole input>}` and one `choice` question with this instruction, written before the dev

3. Jev's label is used when its probability is at least 0.6. Below that, on any error, or on a label outside the

4. The objective is the first ask sentence of the chosen block, the rule D uses.
5. Calls carry the feature tag `lcc_intake_objective_dj` in the Jev ledger, and each result row records the model

8. `run_dj.py` refuses to overwrite a results file, and `--split test` refuses unless D is the frozen D (the check

**The analysis.**

- Intervals are the designed paired cluster bootstrap. Each of 10,000 resamples (seed 20261116, Python's
  `random.Random`) draws as many bases as the split has, with replacement; its statistic is the mean per-case
  difference over every case of the drawn bases. The interval is the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles from
  `statistics.quantiles(n=40, method="inclusive")`; the point estimate is the mean over all cases.
- H1 and H2 as designed. H3 compares point estimates on `first`, as the design writes it. H4 reads D's ask recall
  and rebuild from `run_arms.py`; A0, A1 and DJ change only which sentence is the objective and keep the intake's
  sentence list, so Amendment 2's reading gives them recall 1.00 by construction.
- Reported apart, with no pass criterion: hit@1 by family, length and language; D minus A1; the ablations; the test
  `marker` and `restate` cases whose phrasings Codex wrote (the leakage table); and the test `restate` cases where
  the parser does not label the gold recap sentence `ask` (Amendment 1). The script finds those cases from the
  parser's role of the gold span and never prints or stores their text.

1. The journal: before a case's call its id is synced to disk, and the journal's folder entry before the first
