# Intake dictation v1: can intake cut what a dictation says to someone else without losing an ask? (draft 2026-11-15)

**Status: pre-registered on 2026-11-15, before any arm ran.** The generator, the cases file (sha256 below), the
metrics and the pass criteria are fixed from here. Anything that changes them after a run is a dated amendment at
the end of this file that says what it changes and why. Results are tagged INFERIDO until `gateClaim` returns PASS
on them.

## Why

`lcc intake` (the "compiler") was built for typed prompts and SRT/VTT transcripts. The owner dictates most prompts
by voice, and on 2026-11-15 two of his real pt-BR dictations went through `lcc intake <file> -f json -r report.json`
at `origin/main` `a80eae3`, offline:

1. **56 tokens, ending in "Te amo."**, said to his wife and caught by the dictation. Dictation was not detected,
   the aside was kept, and the objective was the first 80 characters of the message, which is neither of its two
   real asks. Readiness READY 90.
2. **317 tokens of free dictation.** Dictation was detected, but only one ", né," (5 characters) was removed. The
   objective was again the first 80 characters. Readiness NEEDS_INTAKE 40 because "talvez", a hedge, counted as
   ambiguity, and the readiness questions came back generic and in English for a Portuguese message.

Two messages say what fails, not how often. This study measures the failure classes on synthetic dictations whose
answer is known exactly, before and after the change.

What it cannot show: how often real dictations contain asides, corrections or repetitions, or how the rules behave
on another speaker's speech. The noise is injected by the same author who wrote the rules (see Leakage controls).

## Data

Synthetic, generated by `intake_dictation_v1.py generate` with `random.Random(20261115)`:

- **28 clean requests** (`BASES`), 14 in pt-BR and 14 in English, written by hand. Each is a list of sentences
  labelled `ask`, `context` or `constraint`, with one or two asks, and is fully specified on its own (gold
  readiness is never `NEEDS_INTAKE`). One sentence per request holds a correction slot `[[right|wrong]]`.
- **Splits.** Odd-numbered requests (`pt01`, `pt03`, …) are the **dev** split: rules may be read against them.
  Even-numbered requests are the **test** split, and the headline numbers are test numbers.
- **10 variants per request** (280 cases), one per family:

| Family | What is injected | Gold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `clean` | nothing | no cut (false-cut control) |
| `aside_lexicon` | one sentence from the Layer 1 aside lexicon ("Te amo.", "Já vou, amor!", "Love you!", "Coming, honey!") at a random sentence boundary | cut the sentence |
| `aside_open` | one aside **outside** that lexicon: a pet command ("Thor, sai daí!", "Bella, drop it!"), a TV line, phone side-talk | cut the sentence |
| `filler` | two fillers: comma-bounded (", né,", ", you know,"), a sentence opener ("Tipo, ", "Basically, ") or an interjection ("hum, ", "uh, ") | cut the filler |
| `self_correction` | the slot says the wrong value, an editing term, then the right one: "desde segunda, quer dizer, desde ontem", "on Friday, no, I mean, on Monday" | cut the wrong value and the editing term; keep the repair |
| `repetition` | the first one or two words of an ask, said twice: "Preciso, preciso que…", "Fix the fix the…" | cut the first copy |
| `false_start` | a cut-off word before an ask: "Pre- Preciso que…", "Fi- Fix the…" | cut the fragment |
| `distractor` | a sentence that looks like an aside but is content or thinking aloud: "Meu cachorro derrubou o roteador ontem…", the quoted UI label "Te amo", "Let me think how to put this." | keep |
| `vague` | real vagueness: "Não sei se é o melhor jeito, sei lá." | keep (exists so readiness asks questions) |
| `mixed` | three of the first seven noise families, drawn at random (never repetition and false start together) | as above |

- **Cases file:** `cases.json`, 280 cases, sha256
  `dd190cf2743db32a6a332909f5ada533904da8f39bbba813411aad43a8bb82d6`. Every result records the hash it scored.
- Each case stores its text, every gold cut span with its family, the keep spans of each ask, and the span of each
  injected aside, all as character offsets into the text. Deleting the gold cut spans gives the clean request back
  (a test checks this on every case).

## Arms

| Arm | Code | Network |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A0** | `origin/main` `bf8bf02`, unchanged (`git archive` into a private directory, run with `--src`) | none (`LCC_DISABLE_NETWORK=1`) |
| **L1** | this branch, default `LccIntake(optimize_context=False)`: Layer 1 rules and the Layer 3 brief | none |
| **L12** | this branch, `LccIntake(optimize_context=False, judge_roles=True)`: L1 plus the Layer 2 Jev judgment | Jev, synthetic text only |

The owner's two real messages run through A0 and L1 locally. They are sent to Jev (L12) only after the owner says
yes; their text never enters this repository.

### Layer 1 (deterministic, `src/lcc/intake/speech.py`), frozen before the first run

Each rule removes exact spans and records them in the receipt; nothing is inserted or re-cased.

- **Aside lexicon.** A whole sentence (between terminators or line breaks) that is only an endearment or "I'm
  coming" said to someone in the room: pt `te amo`, `também te amo`, `amo você`, `já vou`, `tô indo`, `já tô
  indo`; en `love you`, `love you too`, `i love you`, `coming`, `be right there`, `on my way`; each optionally with
  a vocative before or after (`amor`, `meu amor`, `querida`, `querido`, `vida`, `mãe`, `pai`, `filho`, `filha`;
  `honey`, `babe`, `baby`, `love`, `sweetie`, `mom`, `dad`, `darling`). A sentence that says anything else is not
  matched. A match also counts as a dictation signal.
- **Self-correction.** A comma, an editing term, then the repair. Strong terms (pt `não, quer dizer`, `ou melhor`,
  `digo`; en `no, I mean`, `sorry, I mean`, `no wait`, `or rather`, `scratch that`) and weak terms (pt `quer
  dizer`, `não`; en `I mean`, `no`, `sorry`). When the repair's first word appears again in the up-to-8 words
  before the editing term, within the same clause, everything from that word through the editing term is cut
  ("na sexta, não, quer dizer, na segunda" → "na segunda"). Otherwise, for strong terms only, the single word
  before the editing term is cut ("dez, não, quer dizer, oito" → "oito"), never the first word of a sentence.
  Weak terms with no parallel word cut nothing.
- **Repetition.** The same 2 to 4 words said twice in a row (case-insensitive, optional comma between): the first
  copy is cut. One repeated word is cut only with a comma or hyphen between the copies ("Preciso, preciso"), never
  for words that double as content (`that`, `had`, `is`, `muito`, `very`, `bem`, `so`, `really`, `mais`, `tão`).
- **False start.** A word fragment ending in a hyphen followed by a word that starts with it ("Pre- Preciso"): the
  fragment is cut.
- Existing filler and transcript rules are unchanged. All rules skip code fences and inline code. Question tags
  ("né?", "right?") stay, so no removed span ever contains "?".

### Layer 2 (Jev, opt-in)

One System One request per message (sentences batched by 32), one `choice` question per sentence: `ask`,
`context`, `constraint`, `aside` (words meant for someone or something other than the assistant) or
`thinking_aloud`. A sentence is cut only when `probabilities.aside >= 0.9`. No answer, a malformed answer, no key or
network disabled: nothing is cut and the report says `degraded`. The threshold is fixed here, before L12 runs.

### Layer 3 (extractive brief)

Asks, context and constraints are listed as copies of the input's own sentences after the cuts, each with the
character spans it came from. Roles come from Jev when L12 answered, otherwise from cue words. Readiness questions
are written in the input's language (pt or en, from a stopword count).

## Scoring (mechanical, no judge)

Unit: word tokens (`\w+`) of the case text, with known positions. A token is **gold-cut** when it lies in a gold
cut span, **predicted-cut** when every character of it lies in a removed span of the intake receipt.

## Metrics

Per split (test, dev) and overall:

- **Cut precision, recall, F1** (micro over tokens), recall per family, and false cuts as a share of keep tokens.
- **Ask survival:** share of gold asks whose keep tokens are all still present after the cuts.
- **Brief ask recall:** share of gold asks whose keep tokens appear, in order, inside one ask item of the brief. A0
  has no ask list; its brief objective is its only ask item.
- **Brief ask precision:** share of brief ask items that contain a gold ask that way.
- **Aside leaks:** cases where an injected aside's tokens appear contiguously in any brief item.
- **Questions in input language:** among cases with readiness questions, share whose questions all read as the
  case's language (stopword count in the harness).
- **False NEEDS_INTAKE:** share of non-`vague` cases (all fully specified) marked `NEEDS_INTAKE`.
- **Dictation detected** on noisy cases and on clean cases.
- **Rebuilt byte for byte:** the cleaned text plus the receipt rebuilds the input exactly.
- **Brief spans verbatim:** every brief item equals the concatenation of the input spans it lists.
- **Token reduction** (`lcc.token_budget`, `claude-sonnet-5`, method recorded by the library).

## Hypotheses and pass criteria (declared before any run)

| Id | Claim | Pass |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| H1 | Cuts never remove an ask | ask survival = 1.0 in L1 and L12, both splits. Gate: a rule that breaks it is fixed or removed before any number is reported. |
| H2 | Nothing is cut silently and briefs only copy | rebuilt byte for byte = 1.0 and brief spans verbatim = 1.0 in L1 and L12 |
| H3 | Layer 1 is precise | L1 test cut precision >= 0.95 and false cuts <= 0.01 of keep tokens |
| H4 | Layer 1 finds more than A0 | L1 test recall > A0 test recall on each of `aside_lexicon`, `self_correction`, `repetition`, `false_start` |
| H5 | Jev catches asides outside the lexicon | L12 test recall on `aside_open` >= 0.8 with L12 test cut precision >= 0.95 |
| H6 | The brief carries the real asks | L1 test brief ask recall >= 0.9 and above A0 |
| H7 | Questions follow the input's language | L1 questions in input language = 1.0 |

A hypothesis that fails is reported as failed, with its number. No threshold moves after a run without an amendment.

## Leakage controls

- The phrase lists in the generator and the Layer 1 rules have one author. `aside_lexicon`, `filler`,
  `self_correction`, `repetition` and `false_start` therefore test that the rules work as specified and do not
  break asks; they are not a measure of coverage on real speech.
- `aside_open` is the generalisation check: a test asserts that no `aside_open` phrase matches the Layer 1 lexicon
  and that every `aside_lexicon` phrase does.
- Rules are tuned on dev only. Test numbers are reported as they come; a change made after seeing test numbers is an
  amendment and its before and after test numbers are both reported.

## Amendments

### Amendment 1 (2026-11-15, after the first L1 run, before L12): constraints are not asks

**What changed.** `parse_intake` counts asks (`ask_count`, which drives `voice_dump` and `multiple_asks`) over
the sentences the brief does not mark as constraints. Before, "Não faça merge sem os testes passarem." counted as
one more ask through the cue word "faça".

**Why.** The first L1 run marked 4 fully specified test cases `NEEDS_INTAKE` (false NEEDS_INTAKE 0.0317 against
A0's 0.0), all from request `pt04`: its constraint and an indicative "Ele muda a autenticação" reached the
3-ask threshold, and the new aside and disfluency signals now detected those variants as dictation, which turns
3 asks into `voice_dump`. A constraint limits an ask; it is not a separate one.

**Effect on test numbers** (both reported): false NEEDS_INTAKE 0.0317 → 0.0; cases with questions 18 → 14;
every other test and dev metric identical. This was seen on the test split, so it is reported as a post-hoc
change. L12 ran after it.

### Amendment 2 (2026-11-15, after all arms ran): Codex review fixes, L1 and L12 re-run

**What changed.** A Codex review of the diff found inputs outside the benchmark where the layers cut or
mislabel real content. Fixed, each with a regression test in `tests/test_intake_dictation.py`:

1. Double-quoted text is literal, like code: `Set the label to "go, go".` is no longer a repetition.
2. A sentence a colon hands over is content, not an aside: `Translate this into English:\nTe amo.` keeps it
   (Layer 1 and Layer 2 share the guard `aside_cut_allowed`).
3. Layer 2 cuts only on a well-formed answer (a valid `choice` and a probability); a malformed answer keeps its
   sentence and marks the judgment degraded, and a failed batch voids the cuts of earlier batches.
4. A sentence that opens with an imperative stays an ask when a rule follows it ("Create the login endpoint; it
   must be authenticated."); "The output must be JSON." is still a constraint.
5. `lcc intake --judge-roles` shows a degraded judgment in the terminal summary without `--report`, and the
   judgment records the resolved Jev model instead of the alias.
6. Second review pass: code inside quotes stays protected (code and quotes are scanned separately), and a
   rule in the imperative's own clause is a constraint ("Build must be reproducible." is about the build).

**Why.** Each one cut or hid user content in a case the synthetic set does not contain.

**Effect.** L1 re-run: every metric identical on both splits. L12 re-run twice (runs 2 and 3, the last on the
final code): the test split matched run 1 except brief ask precision (0.9935, 1.0, 0.9935); dev `aside_open`
recall was 0.8349, 0.7523 and 0.8349. The extra runs also turn H5 from one run into three. After fix 6, L1 was
re-run (every per-case record identical) and run 3's Jev answers, as the ledger records them (choice and
confidence; the choice is the argmax, so p(aside) is the confidence when it is "aside" and at most 0.8
otherwise), were replayed through the final code: all 280 L12 records identical.
